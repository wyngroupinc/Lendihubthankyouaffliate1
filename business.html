The Gap

You are not broke. You are early.

It shows up two ways. Most owners live one of them.

If you invoice

The job is done. The invoice went out. Net 30, maybe net 60.

Friday comes anyway. So do the material bills. So does the fuel.

You earned that money. You just cannot touch it yet.

If you get paid on the spot

You get paid the same day. That is not the problem.

The problem is you buy first. The inventory, the truck, the thing that broke on a Tuesday.

The money has to leave before it can come back.

Either way you do what everybody does. You float it on a card. You pay people late and hope nobody quits. You pass on the next job because you cannot cover two at once.

That last one is the one that hurts. Turning down work you could have done.

The Diagnosis

Timing is fixable. The bank just is not reading for it.

A bank starts with your credit score. That score is a story about your past. It does not know the job you finished last week or the invoice sitting in your customer's system right now.

So the bank sees a number. It does not see a business.

What it never opens

  • $38,000 a month moving through the business account

The Cost Of Waiting

Waiting is not free. It just does not send you a bill.

Three deadlines are already running against you. Two of them have dates on them.

1. The job you are turning down

Think of the last job you passed on because you could not cover it up front. Take what you would have cleared on it.

That is what one month of waiting already cost you. It happens again next month, and the month after.

Almost nobody adds it up. It is usually bigger than the funding.

2. December 31. Miss it by a day and you wait a year.

Section 179 lets you write off the full cost of equipment in the year you buy it. The catch is the date. It has to be running by December 31. Not ordered. Not paid for. Running.

Order it in late December and you miss by weeks. The write-off moves to next year and you carry the cost for twelve extra months.

The 2026 limit is $2,560,000, and it starts phasing out past $4,090,000. Most small businesses are nowhere near either number, which is the point: the whole cost comes off this year.

We are not accountants and this is not tax advice. Ask yours what it means for you. Source: section179.org, 2026 figures.

3. Q4, which is already close

October through December is the busiest stretch of the year. Holiday inventory, equipment bought before the deadline above, lenders racing their own year-end numbers.

Every one of those files lands on the same desks yours does. The line is short right now. It will not be in three weeks.

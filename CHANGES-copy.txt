THE PROBLEM THE COPY HAS TO SOLVE
---------------------------------
The merchant just answered sixteen questions. Now a form asks for their
name, business, phone, email, amount and revenue again. The old copy
said "You're almost done. Finish your application" -- which reads as a
bait-and-switch and makes the duplication feel like your mistake.

You cannot hide the retyping. They will notice in two seconds. So the
copy names it first, apologises, and gives a reason that makes it make
sense.

WHAT IT NOW SAYS
----------------
HERO (reassurance only, no ask)
  You're in.
  A funding specialist has your file. If you sent this before 5pm
  Eastern, you will hear from us today.

PANEL (the ask, framed as a shortcut)
  Want an answer today instead of next week?

  Finish your full application below. It takes about five minutes.

  A few questions look the same. Sorry about that. The ones you just
  answered told us if we can help you. This one is the real
  application. It is what your funding partner reads.

  The big one is your bank statements. Add your last three months and
  we can send your file out today. Skip them and we wait until we
  reach you by phone. That is usually two or three days.

  You can still just take our call. This is the fast lane, not a rule.

UNDER THE FORM
  Encrypted. Only used to get you an offer.

CHECKLIST
  You can start without these. Having them nearby is what gets you an
  answer today instead of next week.

WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY
------------------------
- The headline sells the OUTCOME (speed), not the task. Nobody wants to
  "finish an application". They want money this week.
- "Sorry about that" is doing real work. Naming an annoyance before the
  reader does takes the sting out. Pretending it is not happening is
  what makes people angry.
- "The ones you just answered told us if we can help you. This one is
  the real application" reframes the duplication as two different jobs
  rather than one job done twice. It is also true.
- Bank statements are called out on their own, in bold, because they
  are the only field that genuinely changes your speed. Everything else
  is retyping; statements are the deal.
- The cost of skipping is stated plainly (two or three days of phone
  tag) without threatening anyone.
- The last line gives permission to skip. Counter-intuitive, but a
  reader who feels trapped leaves. A reader who feels free stays and
  reads the offer.
- The hero no longer repeats the panel. One job each: hero reassures,
  panel asks.

READING LEVEL (your grade 5 rule)
---------------------------------
  hero                 2.31
  panel headline       3.76
  panel body           2.28
  checklist intro      3.64
  when-we-call card    2.61
  checklist items      4.56
  legal small print    6.78   <- left alone on purpose

Every marketing block is well under 5. The only block over is the
not-an-approval disclaimer, and simplifying legal wording to hit a
readability target is how you end up saying something you did not mean.
